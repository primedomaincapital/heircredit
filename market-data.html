Heir credit, probate and the wealth transfer, by the numbers

Wealth-transfer projections, probate timelines and costs, inheritance-advance pricing, and estate-tax thresholds, from published sources.

Two different kinds of number sit behind this name, and they must not be mixed. The first kind measures the transfer itself: how much wealth is projected to change hands, who it comes from, and who receives it. The second kind measures the wait: how long probate takes, what it costs, and what an heir pays to turn a future distribution into cash now. The first kind tells a reader how large the market is. The second tells a reader why the market exists at all.

What the latest numbers show is a transfer that keeps getting larger on paper while the wait that defines it stays stubbornly long: Cerulli's projection rose from $84 trillion to $124 trillion in three years, while the average probate case still takes twenty months — and only 2% of Americans can say so.

How to read these numbers

Included: Cerulli Associates' 2024 wealth-transfer projections and advisor-retention survey; Trust & Will's 2024 State of Probate study; U.S. Bank's probate guidance; Settled Estate's county-level probate-cost range; LegalClarity and InheritCashNow advance-fee bands; Inheritance Funding Company, TriMark Legal Funding, and Finder advance terms; the 2026 federal estate-tax exemption and state thresholds; and Caring.com's 2025 wills survey. Excluded: any single "average inheritance" headline (published averages vary widely by method and by whether the median or the mean is used); the $2.5-trillion-a-year current inheritance flow and the $4-trillion-around-2036 projection, which circulate in commentary without a primary Cerulli release to anchor them; and any blending of advance-fee percentages with loan interest rates, which describe different products.

Practitioner advance-fee figures are market ranges, not quotes, and are labeled as such wherever they appear. Projections are labeled as projections, not actuals. State probate and tax figures vary by jurisdiction and change over time; confirm the current schedule with the local court or tax authority before relying on a number.
