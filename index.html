The thesis

A name that states the heir and the money.

Every estate has two clocks. One measures what the heir is owed. The other measures when the court will let them have it. Between those two clocks sits a business: advances against an expected inheritance, loans secured by estate assets, and the finance that carries a family through probate.

That is why this name works as a business identity rather than a slogan. An heir reading it knows the subject is money that arrives before the estate closes. A funder reading it knows the subject is a claim on a probated estate, priced for time and risk. A trust officer reading it knows the subject is the liquidity problem their clients keep bringing through the door.

The name is short, pronounceable, and exact. It works on an inheritance-advance firm's homepage, on an estate lender's term sheet, on a trust company's service page, and in front of a family that needs to know, in one glance, that this is not a general loan site. It is an heir site first, and a credit site second.

Specific, and broad enough to travel. A strong heir-credit brand must cover three linked markets at once: the advance itself, the lending that surrounds an estate, and the private credit that funds both. This name covers inheritance advances, probate finance, estate-secured lending, and trust-adjacent credit without narrowing to any single one — and without vagueness that says nothing at all.
