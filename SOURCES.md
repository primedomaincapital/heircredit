# Market-data and resources pages (November 6, 2026)

## market-data.html — figure sources (all figures seen in public page text)

- Cerulli Associates, U.S. High-Net-Worth and Ultra-High-Net-Worth Markets 2024: $124 trillion projected to transfer through 2048 ($105 trillion to heirs, $18 trillion to charity); earlier estimate $84.4 trillion through 2045 ($72.6 trillion to heirs, $11.9 trillion to charity); more than half (about $62 trillion) from high-net-worth and ultra-high-net-worth households, about 2% of households; 81% from Baby Boomers and older generations; about $54 trillion to spouses first, nearly $40 trillion to widowed women; millennials about $46 trillion by 2048, Generation X about $14 trillion in the next decade; equities up 27% and real estate up 39% from 2020 to 2023. As summarized at https://www.masterworks.com/academy/posts/the-great-wealth-transfer-84-trillion-dollars-and-the-next-generation and https://www.timeless.ai/great-wealth-transfer-statistics
- Cerulli Associates press release, "Aging Boomers Bring Intergenerational Planning to the Forefront": more than 70% of heirs likely to fire or change financial advisors after inheriting; only 27% of future beneficiaries plan to retain the benefactor's advisor, 20% among those who have already inherited (survey of investors holding at least $250,000 in financial assets). https://www.cerulli.com/press-releases/aging-boomers-bring-intergenerational-planning-to-the-forefront
- McKinsey research via Medallia: 70% of women who inherit wealth leave their existing advisor within a year of their partner's death; 76% of firms only engage with heirs at the point of transfer; 80% of clients change advisors at the point of inheritance. https://www.medallia.com/blog/how-cx-can-help-financial-institutions-retain-business-during-the-great-wealth-transfer/
- Trust & Will, "The Costs, Timeline, and Emotional Toll of the Probate Process" (July 17, 2024): average probate timeline 20 months; only 2% of respondents estimated it correctly; 15% guessed 1–3 months, 15% guessed 4–6 months, 37% uncertain; average probate cost 3–7% of estate value; survey of 1,000 Americans conducted March 13–18, 2024 with OnePoll. https://Trustandwill.com/learn/2024-probate-study
- U.S. Bank, "What Is Probate?": general estimate 18 months; small simple uncontested estates four to six months in some states; courts expect completion in roughly 18 months; Jonathan Longfield, trust and probate specialist, U.S. Bank Private Wealth Management. https://www.usbank.com/wealth-management/financial-perspectives/trust-and-estate-planning/what-is-probate.html
- Settled Estate, "Probate Costs Explained" (January 15, 2026; reviewed April 11, 2026): probate typically 3% to 8% of gross estate value; filing-fee data across 3,223 counties in 50 states and D.C. https://www.settledestate.com/probate-costs/
- RealCostReport, "Probate Duration by State and Complexity" (2026): creditor claim periods — Florida 3 months, New York 7 months; California 4-month creditor period; Texas 4–12 months via independent administration; real estate adds an estimated 4–8 months. https://www.realcostreport.com/estate-planning/probate/probate-timeline/
- LegalClarity, "How Much Does an Inheritance Advance Cost? Fees Explained" and "Can I Get an Advance on My Inheritance?": fees typically 10% to 40% of the amount received, most transactions 20% to 40%; $10,000 advance typically means assigning $13,000 to $14,000; minimum expected inheritance commonly around $15,000. https://legalclarity.org/how-much-does-an-inheritance-advance-cost-fees-rates/ and https://legalclarity.org/can-i-get-an-advance-on-my-inheritance-how-it-works/
- InheritCashNow, "Estate Cash Advance: How It Works": fee bands 10–15% (3–6 months), 15–25% (6–12 months), 20–30% (12–18 months), 25–40% (18+ months); funds wired within 24–48 hours; advances typically 30–70% of expected inheritance. https://www.inheritcashnow.com/blog/estate-cash-advance
- Inheritance Funding Company, "How Do Inheritance Advances Work?" and Finder review: in business since 1992; BBB accredited since 1995, A+ rating; more than $200 million advanced; advances $5,000 to $350,000, up to 40% of expected inheritance; minimum inheritance $10,000. https://www.inheritancefunding.com/inheritance-advances/ and https://www.finder.com/personal-loans/inheritance-funding-company
- TriMark Legal Funding: in business since 2003; more than $105 million funded; funding in as little as 24 hours. https://tlfllc.com/inheritance-funding
- ProbateLend: advances typically no more than 30–35% of an heir's share. https://probatelend.com/inheritance-cash-advance-companies-what-you-need-to-know/
- LegalClarity, "What Is the Current Estate Tax Exemption Amount?": 2026 federal estate tax exemption $15 million per individual, $30 million for a married couple using portability; made permanent by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed July 4, 2025; inflation adjustments from 2027; rates up to 40% above the exemption; 2025 exemption $13.99 million. https://legalclarity.org/what-is-the-current-estate-tax-exemption-amount/
- TheStreet, "Bank of America flags 5 uses for trusts beyond taxes" (August 29, 2026): twelve states plus D.C. impose estate taxes, five impose inheritance taxes; Oregon $1 million, Massachusetts $2 million, Minnesota $3 million, Washington $3 million for deaths on or after July 1, 2026 with top rate cut from 35% to 20%, New York $7.35 million, Connecticut $15 million; Tax Foundation data. https://www.thestreet.com/taxes/bank-of-america-trusts-obbba-15-million-estate-exemption
- RealCostReport, "Federal Estate Tax Threshold 2026": fewer than 1 in 1,000 estates owe federal estate tax, roughly 0.14% of deaths, per the Tax Policy Center; Congressional Research Service report R48183 on P.L. 119-21. https://www.realcostreport.com/estate-planning/probate/federal-estate-tax-exemption/
- Caring.com, 2025 Wills and Estate Planning Survey (January 31, 2025): only 24% of respondents have a will, down from 33% in 2022; 13% a living trust; survey of more than 2,500 American adults with YouGov, January 3–6, 2025. https://www.caring.com/about/news/2025-will-and-estate-planning-survey
- LegalZoom, "Estate Planning Statistics" (updated February 14, 2026): 43% report not having a will or other estate planning documents because they simply haven't gotten around to it; 56% feel they don't have enough assets to justify creating an estate plan. https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/estate-planning-statistics
- J.P. Morgan Private Bank, Jeff Kreisler, "Tackling the behavioral factors that keep you from making a will" (October 28, 2026): Pew Research Center survey of 8,750 U.S. adults, fewer than one in three have a will. https://www.jpmorganchase.com/newsroom/stories/kreisler-will
- The Wealth Advisor (December 2, 2025): Trust & Will survey of 10,000 U.S. adults — more than 80% recognize the importance of estate planning, only 31% have a will. https://www.thewealthadvisor.com/article/most-people-know-importance-will-less-one-third-actually-have-one
- IRS 2026 figures: annual gift-tax exclusion $19,000 per recipient ($38,000 for a married couple splitting gifts); Form 706 due nine months after death with a six-month extension available. Via the 2026 estate-planning guides cited above.
